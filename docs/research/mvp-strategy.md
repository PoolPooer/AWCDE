# MVP Strategy

## Purpose

This document captures the research strategy for the AWCDE MVP.

It identifies the assumptions that require validation, the evidence needed to support architectural decisions, and the constraints that intentionally limit the scope of the initial implementation.

Unlike the architecture documentation, which describes established design decisions, this document records the reasoning behind those decisions before they are formally adopted.

---

# Guiding Philosophy

The objective of the MVP is not to build a complete AI infrastructure platform.

Instead, the MVP exists to validate a single hypothesis:

> AI inference telemetry can be transformed into a standardized, explainable representation of workload behaviour that helps developers understand benchmark results and make better workload-aware decisions.

Every feature included in the MVP should contribute directly to validating this hypothesis.

Features that do not directly contribute to validating the core hypothesis should be deferred until after the MVP.

---

# Telemetry Provider Selection

Selecting telemetry providers is a research activity rather than an implementation decision.

Candidate providers should be evaluated against objective criteria including:

- Community adoption
- Open source licensing
- Active maintenance
- Repeatability of benchmark methodology
- Availability of structured output
- Breadth of telemetry exposed
- Documentation quality
- Availability of public benchmark datasets
- Ease of integration
- Long-term sustainability

The MVP is expected to support no more than one or two telemetry providers.

Supporting additional providers should only occur once the behavioural model has been validated.

Provider selection should be evidence-driven rather than preference-driven.

The goal is not to support as many providers as possible, but to select providers that collectively provide sufficient behavioural evidence to validate the behavioural model.

---

# Public Benchmark Evidence

The behavioural model should not depend solely on benchmarks generated by the project.

Where appropriate, AWCDE should be capable of interpreting publicly available benchmark results produced using supported benchmarking frameworks.

Potential evidence sources include:

- Academic publications
- Vendor benchmark reports
- Community benchmark repositories
- Open-source benchmark datasets
- User-contributed benchmark results

Generating representative benchmark data across a broad range of models, runtimes and hardware platforms is beyond the scope of a typical research project.

Where appropriate, AWCDE should leverage trusted public benchmark evidence to validate the behavioural model across a significantly wider range of workloads than would be practical through locally generated benchmarks alone.

---

# Benchmark Reference Library

If sufficient public benchmark data is available, future versions of AWCDE may maintain a curated reference library of benchmark results and associated Workload Signatures.

Such a repository could support:

- Behavioural model validation
- Cross-provider comparison
- Community contributions
- Regression testing
- Reference workloads

A benchmark library is outside the scope of the MVP. Such a repository is intended to improve validation of the behavioural model rather than become a benchmarking platform in its own right.

---

# Hardware Context

A Workload Signature describes workload behaviour independently of any specific deployment platform.

Hardware capabilities should instead be treated as deployment context that influences workload-aware guidance.

Future versions of AWCDE may support hardware profiles describing resources such as:

- CPU
- GPU
- Memory
- Storage
- Accelerator capabilities

This information should remain separate from workload characterization.

Separating workload behaviour from hardware capabilities allows the same Workload Signature to be evaluated against multiple deployment environments without regenerating the behavioural model.

---

# Optimization Objectives

The MVP focuses on generating explainable workload understanding.

The Workload Signature should remain independent of deployment objectives.

Different users may seek different outcomes from the same workload, and those objectives should influence workload-aware guidance rather than workload characterization itself.

The Workload Signature describes how a workload behaves.

Optimization objectives describe what the user wants to optimize.

These concerns should remain independent.

- Lowest latency
- Highest throughput
- Lowest operating cost
- Lowest energy consumption
- Highest hardware utilization
- Balanced optimization

These optimization objectives influence deployment guidance but do not alter the underlying Workload Signature.

---

# Privacy Considerations

The MVP should avoid storing identifiable operational information.

Where possible, benchmark evidence should consist only of telemetry required for workload interpretation.

Operational identifiers and environment-specific metadata should be excluded or anonymized unless they are required to support behavioural analysis.

The focus of the platform is workload behaviour rather than operational monitoring.

---

# Open Research Questions

The following questions remain under investigation:

- Which telemetry providers offer the most representative and reusable benchmark evidence?
- What minimum set of telemetry is required to characterize workload behaviour?
- Can publicly available benchmark datasets provide sufficient evidence to validate the behavioural model?
- Which workload characteristics are consistently observable across multiple benchmarking frameworks?
- How should deployment context (hardware, cost, energy efficiency and operational objectives) influence workload-aware guidance without becoming part of the Workload Signature?

# MVP Scope

The MVP intentionally prioritizes validation over feature completeness.

Its purpose is to demonstrate that AI inference telemetry from a limited number of benchmarking frameworks can be transformed into standardized, explainable workload understanding.

Capabilities such as runtime telemetry, hardware profiling, community benchmark repositories, optimization objectives, and production observability are considered future extensions rather than MVP requirements.

# MVP Success Criteria

The MVP will be considered successful if it demonstrates that:

- AI inference telemetry from supported providers can be normalized into a common behavioural model.
- The resulting Workload Signatures remain explainable and traceable to their underlying telemetry.
- Behavioural understanding is reusable across multiple deployment scenarios.
- Workload-aware guidance can be generated without requiring provider-specific reasoning.
- The architecture remains extensible as additional telemetry providers are introduced.
- Existing benchmark telemetry can be interpreted without modifying the original benchmarking frameworks.
- Developers can understand workload behaviour without requiring detailed knowledge of provider-specific benchmark metrics.
